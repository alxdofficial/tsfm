## 1. Positioning / Framing

**+ "Incremental novelty / just a combination of known ideas" (Review#B/C/D). [REBUTTAL: logical-argument + point-to-existing]**

− We agree the ML building blocks — masked autoencoding, channel-independent CNN tokenization, contrastive learning, SBERT embeddings, CLIP-style alignment — are established prior art, and we claim **no new ML primitive** (stated as our "key integration insight," Intro p.2 contribution bullets). HALO's contribution is a **unified OBJECTIVE and the co-design around it**, not a new building block. *The primitives are prior art; the objective is not — HALO optimizes for cross-configuration compatibility and graceful failure, not peak accuracy on one dataset with one fixed sensor layout.*

− Prior IMU models optimize peak accuracy on **one** dataset with a **fixed** sensor format and a **closed** label set; HALO instead optimizes for cross-configuration **COMPATIBILITY** and **graceful degradation** across data heterogeneity (rate/channel/placement) **AND** label heterogeneity (synonyms/paraphrases/unseen vocabularies) — one model ingesting incompatible channel counts (3–51) and rates (20–100 Hz), recognizing activities by open-vocabulary retrieval over arbitrary runtime label banks, with no per-dataset retraining or architectural change. *"It's a combination of known ideas" describes most systems contributions; the relevant test at a sensing venue is whether the combination yields a capability the parts do not — ours does* (points 1.4–1.5).

− The components are **not an arbitrary stack**: each named heterogeneity axis maps **one-to-one** to a co-designed mechanism — rate → seconds-based adaptive-pooling tokenization (Sec 5.1.1); channel/placement → channel-independent processing + per-channel sensor-description conditioning where each channel's description (e.g. "Accelerometer X-axis, waist-mounted phone") enters the network **as language** (ChannelTextFusion, Sec 5.1.2–5.1.3); label/vocabulary → synonym-aware soft-target alignment (Sec 5.2). This mapping is formalized up front as design requirements **R1–R3 (Sec 3.3)** derived from the heterogeneity analysis (Sec 3.1–3.2); the requirements **ARE** the contribution statement. *The formulation is new; we never claimed the parts were.*

− Each pairing is **load-bearing** in the ablation (**Table 6**): removing channel-text fusion costs **−19.6 pp ZS-O** (the single largest zero-shot drop — more than doubling the model buys, Table 8), removing text augmentation costs **−7.0 pp at 1%**, and signal augmentation closes a **9.5 pp** held-out generalization gap invisible on in-distribution validation. *A combination whose every part is necessary, with deltas this large, is a co-design, not an incidental assembly.*

**+ Key novelty / capability statement (Review#B/C/D). [REBUTTAL: point-to-existing]**

− We are the **FIRST** to deliver a **SINGLE deployable IMU model** that recognizes activities via open-vocabulary **cosine retrieval** across mutually-incompatible channel counts (3–51) and sampling rates (20–100 Hz), with **no per-dataset classifier, retraining, or architectural change** (Sec 4, lines 71–78 & 138–141). *In HALO, text does triple duty — channel-conditioning input, open-set classifier, and heterogeneity-tolerant supervision — so the same language interface that enables open-vocabulary recognition is what lets one model span incompatible sensor formats.*

− This is validated **end-to-end on-device**: the iOS case study (**Sec 6.7, Fig 9**) runs **ONE** model across three body placements (pocket/wrist/upper-arm) and correctly recognizes two labels — "Marching", "Jogging" — **introduced only at inference** and never present during on-device personalization, correctly separating "Marching" from "Walking" and "Jogging" from "Running" (**97.5%** over 40 windows, recovering within one window across a mid-session pocket→wrist sensor shift, Sec 6.7 lines 1109–1121). *The on-device demo settles the "is it more than a combination?" question empirically: one model, three placements, two labels never seen during personalization, correctly separated from Walking and Running.*

− No system in our Related Work (**Sec 2**) combines runtime open-vocabulary retrieval **WITH** cross-configuration sensor handling in one deployable model:
  − vs **MOMENT** (general-purpose TS foundation model, 341 M, 10× HALO): no HAR inductive bias, processes a 6-channel input as **six separate passes**; HALO jointly encodes all channels at 35 M and beats it **+13.7 pp ZS-O** (42.0 vs 28.3, Table 3), **5.26× faster** on-device (Table 9). Scale is not the lever — heterogeneity-aware design is.
  − vs **LiMU-BERT / CrossHAR** (self-supervised IMU): improve transferability via masked-reconstruction / contrastive SSL but remain bound to a **fixed channel count, fixed rate, and a dataset-specific closed-world classifier**. HALO removes the per-dataset classifier (cosine retrieval over a runtime-swappable label library) and the fixed-rate/fixed-channel constraint.
  − vs **LanHAR** (text-aligned HAR): closest text-alignment precedent, but (i) lacks SSL sensor pretraining and (ii) assumes a **fixed sensor configuration**; the paper notes naive text alignment without a heterogeneity-robust encoder "proves insufficient for robust zero-shot transfer." HALO's delta is the heterogeneity-aware encoder **plus** synonym-aware soft supervision — text **conditions** the channels, not just classifies them.
  − vs **ImageBind / CLIP**: bind IMU into a shared space but are not designed for variable sensor configurations; HALO's move beyond CLIP-style alignment is making text the **conditioning signal** for variable channel layouts (ChannelTextFusion), not merely the classification target.
  − vs **concurrent 2025 IMU-language / heterogeneous-sensor work** (SensorLM, IMU2CLIP-style, UniMTS [38], One-Model-class): concurrent rather than prior art — we will cite and differentiate. The defensible delta is the **specific coupling** (per-channel text-as-identity that simultaneously powers open-vocabulary retrieval) plus an end-to-end on-device deployment (Sec 6.7); UniMTS targets heterogeneous configs via unified **pretraining** but does not couple text-as-channel-identity to a runtime label-bank swap, and prior text-IMU work typically fixes the sensor format and does not demonstrate runtime-unseen labels across placements on a phone.

− *The deployable capability is the deliverable; the primitives are the means — and a "just a combination" critique does not reach it. ## 3. Design Rationale

We thank the reviewers for the detailed engagement with our design choices. We group the design questions below; ~80% of our answers point to content already in the submission.

**+ Memory queue vs synonym-aware soft targets — does the FIFO queue reintroduce the synonym conflict? (Review#A). [REBUTTAL: point-to-existing]**

− We agree precisely with the reviewer's diagnosis, and confirm it is **not** the case. Semantic (text–text) similarity is computed **only over the in-batch matrix** M = Z_text·Z_textᵀ, **never** over the queue (Sec 5.2.2, "z-score-normalize the in-batch similarity matrix M = Z_text·Z_textᵀ"). The submission already states the exact split the reviewer asks about: "**Queue entries serve as hard negatives. The blended target P combines soft in-batch targets T with hard one-hot targets for queue entries**" (Sec 5.2.2). So in-batch supervision is **purely soft** — synonyms in the same micro-batch receive partial target mass ("walking"/"strolling" share ~0.4; "walking"/"cycling" <0.01) — and only the 256-entry FIFO queue supplies **hard negatives**, raising effective negatives from 32 to 288 (Sec 5.2.2).

− Why this does not reintroduce the conflict: the soft objective exists to fix **contradictory gradients when synonymous labels co-occur in one micro-batch** (Sec 5.2). With a 32-sample micro-batch drawn across 10 datasets and an 87-label vocabulary, the probability that two synonymous strings co-occur in the **same** batch **and** one is simultaneously queue-resident is low; and because the queue holds **embeddings** (not the in-batch soft-target rows), it can only act as additional negatives — it **cannot overwrite the in-batch soft targets**. The reviewer's worst case ("queue holds walking & strolling") is a rare, bounded perturbation on the negative set, not a contradiction injected into the supervised in-batch targets.

− The three-way comparison (no-queue / hard-negative queue / semantic-aware queue) is a clean ablation we can add in a revision; it is the **substance of this clarification**, not a result we present here (venue rebuttal rules preclude new tables). We note the deployed configuration is the hard-negative-queue variant, and the soft-target path itself is already shown load-bearing (**−7.0 pp at 1%** when the synonym-handling path is removed, Table 6).
  − ⚠ **Tag note (author-only):** P1 queue ablation = REVISION deliverable; the queue=hard-neg / in-batch=soft design point answers R-A directly from the existing text.

**+ Why soft targets rather than hard one-hot targets? (Review#A/C). [REBUTTAL: point-to-existing]**

− This is an **architectural** answer, not a heuristic, and the rationale is in the submission. Standard CLIP-style hard targets "forc[e] synonymous labels apart" (Sec 5.2.2); because "'walking,' 'strolling,' and 'ambulating' describe the same motion, yet standard contrastive objectives treat them as unrelated negatives, producing contradictory gradients when both appear in a batch" (Sec 5.2). When a batch drawn from 10 independently-curated taxonomies contains two strings for the same motion, **hard targets are internally inconsistent** — they demand that two embeddings of the same activity be simultaneously a positive (vs its own IMU) and a maximal negative (vs the other label's IMU). Soft targets weight by text–text semantic similarity, "converting synonym conflict from a source of gradient noise into a learning signal that reinforces semantic structure" (Sec 5.2.2).

− We also already justify the **adaptive sharpening** the reviewer's "soft-target construction" comment targets: raw text–text similarities cluster in 0.4–0.9 and yield near-uniform targets that collapse the space, so we z-score-normalize and sharpen with τ_s=0.5 (Sec 5.2.2) — i.e., the construction is empirically motivated by the failure mode (space collapse) it avoids, not an arbitrary choice.

− A direct hard-target ablation isolating this is straightforward to add in a revision; the **−7.0 pp** text-augmentation result (Table 6) is the existing evidence the synonym-handling pathway is necessary.
  − ⚠ **Tag note (author-only):** P7 hard-targets ablation = REVISION deliverable; the contradictory-gradient argument answers R-A/R-C from existing text.

**+ Why an architectural solution rather than simpler post-processing / label normalization? (Review#C/D). [REBUTTAL: point-to-existing + logical-argument]**

− *The combination critique and the label-normalization critique have the same answer.* Two prongs, both already in the paper:

− **(1) Post-hoc label mapping presupposes a fixed, closed label set and per-dataset engineering.** A normalization table maps an argmax **after** prediction over a frozen vocabulary; it cannot admit a label string unseen at training time, and it must be re-authored for every new deployment vocabulary. By contrast, HALO's inference is "cosine-similarity retrieval over an arbitrary text library, so the candidate set can be changed at runtime without retraining" (Sec 3.2; Sec 4), and "no per-dataset classifier is needed" (Sec 4) — the open-vocabulary capability is **structural**, not a lookup applied afterwards.

− **(2) The heterogeneity problem is upstream of labels — post-processing cannot fix the representation.** The submission argues effective text alignment "requires both (i) a sensor encoder robust to the heterogeneity described above, and (ii) a training objective that handles synonym variation" (Sec 3.2). Label normalization addresses only (ii)'s symptom and does nothing for (i): under rate/channel/placement shift the **embedding is wrong before any label is assigned**, so no post-hoc string remap can recover it. Further, our test sets contain "activities with no semantic analog in the training label set (e.g., car_step_in in MobiAct, industrial activities in VTT-ConIoT), making correct predictions **structurally impossible for any closed-world model**" (Sec 3.2) — precisely the cases a normalization table cannot reach, because there is no fixed target row to map them to. *You cannot post-process your way out of a representation that was never built to ingest a 51-channel body-suit and a 3-channel phone with the same weights — heterogeneity is upstream of labels.*

− We scope the claim carefully: simple walking-vs-jogging **synonymy** could indeed be partly handled by normalization, and we agree — but that is the **easy** sub-problem. HALO targets the **joint** case where the label space is open **and** the sensor configuration shifts, which is where post-processing fails. [REBUTTAL: logical-argument]

**+ Is HAR label variation complex enough to justify the synonym focus, and does textual similarity even track sensor-dynamics similarity? (Review#D). [REBUTTAL: logical-argument + point-to-existing]**

− We concede the reviewer's sharper point **partially**: textual similarity is **not** guaranteed to equal sensor-dynamics similarity (two semantically-adjacent labels can be kinematically distinct). We do not claim it always does. What we claim — and already show empirically — is that for the HAR label vocabularies in question the learned text-conditioned space **does** recover kinematic structure. **Fig 6 / Sec 6.3.3**: IMU↔text nearest-neighbor accuracy is **76.7%**, positive (matched) similarity **0.857**, matched-vs-unmatched gap **0.377**; "semantically related groups (e.g., running, stairs, walking) cluster together" while "stationary activities form a distinct cluster separated from locomotion, mirroring kinematic structure." That is direct evidence the text-induced geometry **aligns with sensor dynamics rather than fighting it** in our setting.

− On complexity: the synonym problem is non-trivial precisely because each of the 10 training datasets "defines its own taxonomy independently" (Sec 3.1–3.2), averaging 2.3 synonyms per activity over 87 labels (Sec 5.2.1), and the conflict is empirically load-bearing (**−7.0 pp at 1%** without the synonym path, Table 6) — removing the synonym-aware machinery measurably hurts transfer, which would not happen if the problem were trivially solvable by a static map.

**+ Limitations we identify (acknowledging Review#E's constructive remedies; Review#A). [REBUTTAL: logical-argument]**

− We agree the framing should be tempered to "effective under **moderate** cross-dataset heterogeneity, not yet solving extreme sensor/domain shift," and we identify the same remedy directions the reviewer names as open **limitations of the present design**: per-sample sensor metadata (vs our current dataset-level descriptions, whose limit we already observe on RealWorld, **−9.9 pp** with global descriptions, Sec 6.3.2), small calibration sets, domain-adaptive pretraining, and uncertainty-based rejection for out-of-vocabulary inputs. We present these strictly as limitations of the current design, **not** as promised work.

**+ Closing novelty reinforcement (scoped to the Design components) (Review#B/C/D). [REBUTTAL: logical-argument]**

− We are the first to deliver a single model whose **training objective itself** resolves cross-dataset label-synonym conflict via text-derived soft targets while its **encoder** absorbs heterogeneous channel/rate configurations — a co-design where the language space simultaneously supplies the **supervision signal** (soft targets), the **runtime classifier** (label-bank retrieval), and the **conditioning input** (sensor descriptions). No separate-solution baseline (LiMU-BERT/CrossHAR: SSL but closed-world; LanHAR: text-aligned but fixed sensors, no SSL) achieves this combination, and our ablations show each co-designed component is load-bearing (Table 6; ChannelTextFusion removal is the single largest drop in the ablation suite). *Each heterogeneity axis maps to one co-designed mechanism: rate→adaptive tokenization, channel/placement→channel-independent + sensor-description conditioning, label/vocab→synonym-aware soft alignment.* (Primary novelty argument lives in §1; this is a one-line Design-section reinforcement, supported by the no_channel_text 62.4% vs headline 72.0% canary as REVISION-only substance.)
